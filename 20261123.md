So the most pressing issue is why are we wearing out nozzles so fast?

Wearing out a nozzle really fast but an electrode no faster than normal is apparently evidence of
"double-arcing", where the arc jumps from the electrode to the nozzle and then from the nozzle
to the workpiece, instead of being confined by the air flow.

So that could mean not enough airflow?

This paper https://cdn.intechopen.com/pdfs/20904/InTech-On_the_double_arcing_phenomenon_in_a_cutting_arc_torch.pdf identifies
"too large arc current, too small gas mass flow, or a nozzle with a too small bore diameter or with a too large length"
as possible reasons for double-arcing.

And:

> double-arcing is very undesirable; since the arc roots on the nozzle wall
> (especially that corresponding to the arc originated from the cathode) usually destroy the
> nozzle almost instantaneously

Double-arcing could also explain why I've seen the jet flickering around, it could sometimes be coming out
straight and sometimes coming off one side of the nozzle.

I reckon this is what is happening.

OK, update: double-arcing is not actually possible here. Double-arcing is where the arc jumps from the electrode to the nozzle
and then from the nozzle to the workpiece. But since my nozzle is permanently wired to the same electrode as the workpiece,
there would be no voltage left to jump to the workpiece if this were happening.
