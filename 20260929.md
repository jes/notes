## Steering mechanisms

I had a quick walk around the Haynes Motor Museum again today, this time paying particular attention
to front axle arrangements.

The most common arrangement on older cars is to have one track rod linking the left and right uprights, and then a separate rod
connecting the steering gearbox to just one side. This is usually either moving left-and-right roughly parallel with the main
track rod, or forwards-and-backwards roughly perpeindcular to it. In some cases it is at random other angles, anything is fine.

Sometimes the steering rod connects to one end of the main track rod with a ball joint, and sometimes it connects directly to one
of the uprights.

Usually the main track rod passes underneath the leaf springs.

The 1900 Clement Voiturette has an interesting rack-and-pinion arrangement:

It is only connected to one of the uprights. I'm unclear on exactly how the rack is pushed against the pinion, it appears to just
be floating in free space.

And this 1903 American car has the steering mechanism mounted directly to the beam axle:

https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6720

I had considered doing this kind of thing to eliminate bump steer but probably don't care. I think packaging is a more important consideration.

Instead of making a telescoping shaft to isolate the suspension movement from the tiller, they have a flexure, pretty good. Apparently the flexure
was unreliable and prone to breaking though, swings and roundabouts.

I also wondered about using a chain and sprocket to make a steering rack, on the basis that a chain is really just a flexible rack.
