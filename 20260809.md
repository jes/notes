## Keyboard Glockenspiel

The current status on this is that I can't get the tone bars in the right places because the offset on the tone bar holders
is wrong, or the holes are drilled in the wrong places so they won't line up even if correct.

So I tried each tone bar in turn in its correct place and eyeballed how far out it was:

https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6662

The pegs on the tone bar holders are never too close together, they range from correct to about 2mm too far apart.

So the plan is to 3d print a range of variations and then hand-pick them to make each tone bar fit properly. Unsure whether I
want to go to the trouble of somehow labelling them with their offset, or just wing it? They will be practically indistinguishable
when finished so it will be very annoying if I one day pull it apart and have to put it back together by trial and error. But
maybe I don't care, and plan not to do that?

I also experimented with putting silicone o-rings on the pegs to isolate the vibration from the plastic, and it looked to be
a winner. The note rings for longer and sounds less like a "thunk". Even with an o-ring above and below the bar on each peg, to
hold it in place, it still rings nicely and doesn't damp the sound.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u03MNqwkGa0

I did note that the o-rings were tighter on the pegs than the holders are in the wood, so when I tried to lift the tone bar off
it pulled the holders out with it. So probably I will add a little feature to the holder where it fits inside the wood to make it
just a light interference fit in one spot, to hold them in a bit better.

If my new holders are 0mm longer, 0.5mm longer, and 1.0mm longer, how many of each do I need? Well let's look at the total
offset required across a pair of pegs.

 * 0mm: 2
 * 0.5mm: 3
 * 1.0mm: 8
 * 1.5mm: 13
 * 2.0mm: 5

And we make these with:

 * 0mm: 2x 0mm
 * 0.5mm: 0mm + 0.5mm
 * 1.0mm: 2x 0.5mm
 * 1.5mm: 0.5mm + 1.0mm
 * 2.0mm: 2x 1.0mm

So in total per overall offset we need:

 * 0mm: 4x 0mm
 * 0.5mm: 3x 0mm + 3x 0.5mm
 * 1.0mm: 16x 0.5mm
 * 1.5mm: 13x 0.5mm + 13x 1.0mm
 * 2.0mm: 10x 1.0mm

So in total per 3d print we need:

 * 0mm: 7
 * 0.5mm: 32
 * 1.0mm: 23

And that adds up to 62 which is a good sanity check.
