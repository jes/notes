## Tube bending

A good bending former design here: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R7y5MIux_uQ

So that is a plasma cut plate top and bottom, with 3d-printed form in between, made of 2 parts so printed
without overhangs. Has a bush through the middle for the pivot, and has a little clamp thing at the side to
hold the tube in the former.

They are using a fancy hydraulic bender, I am thinking I would clamp the tube to the former and then
have a lever centered on the centre of the former, with a roller on it, that I will pull around the former to
bend the tube.

I need to put a 50&deg; 200mm radius bend in 35mm diameter 2mm thickness steel tube.

Here's a start: https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6823

So there are 2 metal plates and 2 3d-printed formers for each of the main large former and the roller.

They'll be bolted together each with 6x M10 nuts and bolts.

The roller takes a 608 bearing in the plate on each side. I'm guessing the plasma-cut surface won't be ideal
for this but probably just give it a go, can always machine it if necessary.

The main large former just has a 60mm hole all the way through the plates and the 3d prints, may change the
diameter on this to suit whatever short piece of bush I can find.

Then we need a big lever for the roller, with clearance for the M10 nuts and bolts.

And need some plan for how to clamp the tube and main large former to each other and to the bench.
And then done?

The line marks on the main large former plates are to show the 0&deg; nad 50&deg; positions, to give an
idea of when the bend is at the right angle.

ChatGPT review is:

1. Probably needs at least 550 Nm of torque, maybe more like 1000. Works out to about 60kg on a 1.5m lever, which is massive.
2. 608 bearings may not be suitable for the roller due to the enormous load, but because of low speed can just use a bush instead.
3. PLA probably preferable to ABS-GF due to higher stiffness and hardness.
4. The roller will try to pull the tube around the former, and the clamp needs to prevent it from slipping.

Point 4 seems wrong because in order to come around the former it would have to bend, no?

I was thinking of using 608 bearings on the roller to reduce the friction so that it is easier to bend. I think I'll just try it,
if it damages the bearings then I probably don't even care as long as it makes my 2 bends.

In a way it is encouraging that the loads required to bend this tube are so high because that means it won't bend when it's
on the car.

One annoying thing is that I can't bolt the lower plate down to something without blocking the path of the lower
arm of the lever. What if the lever only had one arm? Would that work or would it just bend?

I guess I can 3d print the roller parts now while I think about it, the roller is unlikely to need to change.

I am wondering about bolting this down to the workbench to use it. I don't currently have any holes in the workbench,
which I like, but maybe if I can't think of anything else large and sturdy to use, putting holes in the bench is
