## Plasma cutter current measurement

I connected up the bench supply to the work return clamp and measured the voltage drop from the clamp end to the other end
at various currents:

https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6831

Ohm's law says this should be a straight line and we get:

https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6832

Which is *absolutely* spot on. I didn't expect it to be this perfect.

We get about 0.006 Ohms for the cable, does that sound believable to ChatGPT? Yes, apparently pretty much fits a 6 sq. mm conductor
2 metres long, good.

At 30 amps this would drop 180 mV which is easy to measure with the multimeter, apparently the HF start can cause high voltage
transients that may damage the meter but can just roll the dice on that.

So next thing is to measure this while it is "cutting" and see what it says.

I pulled back the insulation on the work return plug to clip a lead on, and clamped the multimeter to the shelf so that both leads
would reach their respective ends: https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6834

With cutting current set to about 20 on the dial, I meaured about 118 mV drop across the work return cable, which works out to 19.7 amps, so basically spot on.

So at 250 mm/min with a measured 20 amps cutting current, it didn't cut through 3mm steel. It's the lower cut here: https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6833

The nozzle very close to the work, less than 1 mm.

So, very encouraging that it is delivering the required amount of current, that's basically all I need from it.
Still a bit sus about how much mains current it is drawing
but bot says that could be due to the power factor? My AC clamp meter is apparently "true RMS" but unsure how that affects it.

Oh, also still need to measure the voltage at the plasma cutter plug, the wiring may be dropping the voltage too much causing it
to draw more current to compensate, check that next.

Assuming the excessive current draw is just down to bad wiring, then I can give it a better circuit and a better torch and we should
be in business.

There's an instantaneous drop to like 120 volts or something but steady-state drop is from 244v to 236v so probably no big deal there.

I am tempted to measure the AC current draw again. It is possible that when I measured it last time the torch-to-work distance was
excessively high so it needed an excessively high voltage to sustain the cutting current?

With cutting current set to 20 amps it was initially drawing about 20A from AC on pilot arc or when approaching the workpiece,
then 16 or 17 amps with arc correctly transferred. I turned cutting current up to 30 amps and it drew 22A from AC after transferring
arc, possible that it was more than that before transferred, unsure. I think this is still higher than we'd expect?

So we know:
