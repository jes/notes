So we have:

 * the very first job worked straight away
 * all subsequent jobs appeared to reset the controller as soon as the arc is lit by CNC
 * arc lighting by hand always works
 * the first time I tried "shielding" the torch cable with a steel plate in free space it worked straight away
 * all subsequent attempts reverted to resetting the controller

Note I don't actually *know* that the controller is resetting, I just know that the gcode sender reports having
become disconnected and motion stops and the torch relay switches off. This is all *consistent with* the ESP32 resetting
but not definitely proven.

I did have the aluminium foil shielding all around the torch cable on all my previous successful uses of the machine, but
I took that off as part of moving it to the new location because I thought it wasn't needed. Probably I want to put

So how about:

OK, new findings:

 * `$System/Stats` reports nothing of value, will prob need to hack firmware to find out if true reboot
 * I removed the alarm input and the bodge wires
 * M3 in MDI still resets it
 * weirdly, setting torch disable, and then M3 in MDI, and then flicking torch enable, seems to not reset it *as often* as just enabling it directly with M3, unsure why this would make any difference
 * shorting the contacts on the relay output still resets it about the same
 * pulling one wire out of the screw terminal and touching it to the other screw terminal still resets it about the same
 * but pulling both wires out of the screw terminals and touching them together doesn't reset it at all!

Even if I hold the two wires right next to the screw terminals when I touch them together, touching them together (thereby lighting the arc)
doesn't cause any problem.

So the problem is if the arc is lit by the CNC-operated torch trigger cable *while at least one of the terminals is connected
to the relay board*. WTF?

But this is if anything confirming the suspicion that the interference is coming in via the trigger cable. So I think we are still looking
at shielding the trigger cable and probably the torch cable.

ChatGPT suggests the interference is coupling into the control electronics through the inside of the relay, quite funny.

> The relay contacts are galvanically isolated, but they have parasitic capacitance to the coil, PCB tracks and surrounding circuitry.

ChatGPT suggests a ferrite on the trigger cable as close to the relay board as possible. However we know that ferrites do nothing
and are a scam so I am inclined to skip this.

ChatGPT ideas include:

 * ferrite on trigger cable near relay board
 * shield the trigger cable (but won't help if the trigger cable interference is picked up inside the plasma power supply)
 * move the relay board closer to the plasma power supply and operate it with some isolated control signal (how?)

Also, as a diagnostic: do the test where we leave one wire connected to the relay but tap the other wire on the screw terminal,
but this time disconnect the relay from the ESP32. If it now doesn't reset then we have confirmed that the interference is going
through the relay into the ESP32. (But haven't we pretty much confirmed that anyway by noting that disconnecting the wire makes
it not reset?).
