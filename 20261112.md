OK:

I left it for a good probably 7 hours and tried it again, it did *not* start working automatically.

I looked inside the nozzle and all looked well.

Using the manual torch trigger it works fine, doesn't seem to cause any problems.

I shifted the water tray out of the way a bit and made no difference, but didn't manage to fully remove it because it
catches on the torch.

I then tried holding a piece of steel plate in free space in between the torch cable and the control box and it
worked first time! But unable to repeat.

So we have:

 * the very first job worked straight away
 * all subsequent jobs appeared to reset the controller as soon as the arc is lit by CNC
 * arc lighting by hand always works
 * the first time I tried "shielding" the torch cable with a steel plate in free space it worked straight away
 * all subsequent attempts reverted to resetting the controller

Note I don't actually *know* that the controller is resetting, I just know that the gcode sender reports having
become disconnected and motion stops and the torch relay switches off. This is all *consistent with* the ESP32 resetting
but not definitely proven.

I did have the aluminium foil shielding all around the torch cable on all my previous successful uses of the machine, but
I took that off as part of moving it to the new location because I thought it wasn't needed. Probably I want to put
that back on and see if it helps, and if so buy some proper shielding sheath or whatever.

But I am confused as to why it seems fine if I squeeze the trigger manually. Maybe I just need to use shielded cable
for the torch trigger output? I almost did that initially and I do have some but thought I'd test it with the cheaper cable
just to see if it worked and it did.

So how about:

 * if the torch cable is shielded then it contains interference enough to work
 * but if the torch cable is not shielded then the torch trigger cable picks up interference and brings it inside the controller cabinet where it causes the ESP32 to reset

But why did it work the very first time I tried it? And why did it work that one time with steel plate in free space? Would love
to have an explanation for these. It's extremely suspicious to me that it worked specifically the *first* time. If it worked 1 time
out of 50 but it was a random one I wouldn't mind, but why the *first*? I guess I can accept it working the first time I held up the
steel plate. If it was just working at random and I tried 1 new thing each time then of course it started working the first time I
tried that new thing and didn't work subsequently, that's fine. But why did it work the first time in the new place? Just random
chance??
