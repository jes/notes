## Plasma cutter

I have some fresh nozzles and some steel sheet in 1,2,3,4,5 mm thickness.

So let's start with 1mm sheet and work our way up and see what we learn.

The methodology is to make a bunch of straight line cuts to work out what sort of settings we want,
and then cut out a heart shape and verify that it works.

Using the pilot arc disconnecting circuit, with 0.1 second delay for pilot arc disconnection. So pierce
time is 0.1 second longer than whatever is in "G4 P..." for the pierce delay.

### 3mm

Again used 3mm spacer for standoff.

These are kind of dog shit?

Bad bevel, no speed that cuts remotely cleanly.

I went with 600 mm/min to try to cut out a heart.

Notably, the first attempt at cutting out a heart reset the microcontroller on the first attempt at arc ignition.

Then I tried to do a cut but it didn't work very well, I could see it was double-arcing. You can see that there is the
main plasma jet coming out of the orifice and another bright portion coming off the nozzle body itself. It also made
a bad sound as if the arc kept cutting out or something, I don't know if that's actually what was happening but intuitively
sounded like that.

It was obvious that the standoff was really excessive.

I think what happened is my slat table isn't level and the location of the heart was far enough from my initial setup
that the standoff had increased. Also I had obviously removed the sheet and re-loaded it after making the line cuts,
and didn't ensure that it still ended up at the same height.

I looked at the nozzle and it was pretty badly worn so I replaced it with a fresh one and tried to cut out another
heart with 3mm standoff established with the spacer.

The controller was now in the state where all the WiFi diagnostics are green but the sender is stuck in "CONNECTING" for
30 seconds or whatever. Just have to wait for it to repair itself. I am starting to suspect that this is a problem on the
Linux side because rebooting the controller doesn't seem to fix it. Ignore this for now.

Even with the accurate-ish 3mm standoff and a fresh nozzle it was visibly double-arcing. I took a picture

---

So, so far today we've learnt:

 * this machine is really happy cutting 1mm sheet and OK with 2mm sheet, thicker sheets are more tricky
 * torch-to-work distance actually is quite important
 * machine is definitely double-arcing in some situations, possibly only due to excessive standoff

And cutting parameters that have worked are:

 * **1mm steel:** P0.25 F4000
 * **2mm steel:** P0.5 F1000
 * **3mm steel:** P0.6 F600

But the 3mm result is kind of marginal.

Apparently the Hypertherm troubleshooting guide https://www.hypertherm.com/resources/system-support/maintenance-and-use/consumable-care-and-optimization/extending-parts-and-consumables-life/ says:

 * Internal nozzle damager: potentially insufficient air pressure or flow within the plasma chamber.
 * External nozzle damage: potentially incorrect torch height or piercing spatter.
 * Nozzle damage without electrode damage: characteristic of double-arcing.
 * Damage to both: potentially an electrode or gas flow problem.

We have external nozzle damage without significant electrode damage, so this is characteristic of double-arcing, potentially simply due
to incorrect torch height.

I was really hoping to be able to use a large torch height so as to reduce damage from piercing, maybe I am going to need a motorised Z.
It would be good to be able to pierce from a large-ish standoff, then move clear of the piercing mess, then drop to the ideal cut height.

Annoyingly, that does introduce another variable that may need tuning.
