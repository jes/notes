## Plasma cutter

OK, some good progress today...

I tried running at reduced current setting but it made things worse.

I tried soldering up a worn 1.1mm nozzle and re-drilling it to 0.8mm, but again it sucked.

https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6859

Unclear how long
the solder actually lasted though, I looked at it after less than 30 seconds of cutting and it was completely
gone.

I then tried a fresh 1.1mm nozzle and it seemed to be an improvement, and moving it closer to the work
a further improvement, maybe 1mm above work. This test cut had all of the slag on one side, so actually
separated the material, at 300 mm/min.

So then I cut out a heart just to see what would happen.

The top side looked good: https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6857

But the bottom side again was completely welded together: https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6856

Interestingly the parts where it has just crossed a slat seems to have a lot less slag,
perhaps it sticks to the slat instead of to the work?

So at this point I was pretty disappointed, but I whacked it with a hammer just on the off chance,
and it broke free really easily! And all the slag chipped off easily with a hammer and chisel!

Top side: https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6855

Bottom side: https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6854

And after a couple of strokes with a file you would never know all this slag was left on it. So, great success!
A new personal best in 5mm steel!

In fact I would say that the "slag welds the cut back together" problem is kind of a non-issue if it is this easy to remove,
the greater problem is that there is still a parallelogram kerf in some directions.

I understand that the bevel in the cut is a much exaggerated version of torch misalignment, so it could just be that the
torch isn't quite square.

It may just be a case of making an adjustable torch mount.

But in any event, I'm pretty happy with this. This is now making parts from 5mm steel that look good enough to make the
tube roller.

Emma has an idea for a heart/4-leaf-clover shape she wants me to cut out, so I will probably have a go at that first, and if
that looks good then try to make the tube roller parts.

Things are looking up?? In stark contrast to the stock market.

So these "good" parameters are:

 * 1.1mm nozzle in P80 torch
 * 5mm hot-rolled steel plate
 * about 1mm torch-to-work distance (yeah abusive during pierce but who cares)
 * 300 mm/min
 * full 30A current setting
 * about 4 bar pressure during cutting

Now that I have a straight torch I can reverse the torch in the clamp and see if the bevel reverses, that may be worthwhile.

## Buying steel plate

Previously I have bought sheet/plate steel from TheMetalStore and RapidMetals.

Is this actually the most cost-effective way? What other options are there?

I want to buy some sheets of steel in 1mm to 5mm thickness for use on the plasma cutter.

What size do I want?

If we say 400x300, and 1 sheet for each of those 5 thicknesses, how much does that come to including VAT and shipping etc.? And can
ChatGPT find a cheaper way to do it?

I'm not fussy about the exact type of steel and whether it is hot-rolled or cold-rolled.

Bot suggests that 500x500 may be more cost effective as it is commonly stocked, annoyingly that is too big to fit
on my water table in one direction.

### The Metal Store

They cut to size but you still pay for the original sheet. Smallest suitable size is 500x500.

Prices incl. VAT but excl. shipping:

 * 1mm: £6
 * 2mm: £10.50
 * 3mm: £16.50
 * 4mm: £24.00
 * 5mm: £30.00
 * Total: £87.00 plus shipping

And after that I will still have to cut it down to fit on the water table. Probably a non-starter.

### Rapid Metals

They cut to size and price seems to be proportional to area.

So prices excl. VAT and excl. shipping:

 * 1mm (cold-rolled): £3.00
 * 2mm (cold-rolled): £4.68
 * 3mm (cold-rolled): £7.20
 * 4mm (hot-rolled): £10.56
 * 5mm (hot-rolled): £11.52
 * Total: £36.96
 * Total incl. VAT: £44.35

So the shipping would have to be very expensive to come out worse than Rapid Metals.

Cheapest shipping option is £11.00 plus VAT, for an overall total of **£57.55**.

Now on to ChatGPT suggestions: Flash Metals and Metals4u.

### Flash Metals

They cut to size and appear to charge by surface area, with no extra cutting charge.

Prices incl. VAT and excl. shipping:

 * 1mm (cold-rolled): £3.40
 * 2mm (cold-rolled): £6.78
 * 3mm (hot-rolled): £10.18
 * 4mm (hot-rolled): £12.20
 * 5mm (hot-rolled): £15.26
 * Total incl. VAT: £47.82

Very close to Rapid Metals, how is shipping?

Shipping is £12.00 plus VAT, for total of **£62.22**.

Close but more expensive.

### Metals4U

They don't offer 4mm sheets of steel, so I'd have to just not design anything for 4mm thickness.

So far on the cyclecar the leaf spring shackles are 4mm, but they could easily be 5mm with no ill effect,
so I guess score Metals4U based on 2x 5mm sheets and see how it looks.

Prices incl. VAT:

 * 1mm: £3.43
 * 2mm: £7.89
 * 3mm: £11.38
 * 5mm: £20.85, x2
 * Total incl. VAT: £64.40

So more expensive than Flash Metals and Rapid Metals without even looking at shipping, and can't supply 4mm sheet.

So **Rapid Metals** is the winner for this order, good to know. Flash Metals also good to be aware of but just slightly
more expensive this time.

ChatGPT points out I may be better off getting offcuts from local-ish engineering businesses, this may also be a good
way to talk to people in the engineering trade, although easier to just click and buy online so I'll do that for now.
