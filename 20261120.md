## Scale model of solar system

If we were to put a scale model of the solar system along the fence in the garden, where would things end up?

We have 128m if starting at the road and ending at the bottom of the garden or 98m if only the grass, or 104m
if starting at the back of the house, or 80m if starting at the grass and ending at the solar panels.

I think the 80m version is more natural.

So where would things be?

Neptune is the furthest, average distance about 30.2AU, 80m, so 0.378 AU/metre.

So:

 * Sun: 0AU, 0m
 * Mercury: 0.45AU, 1.19m
 * Venus: 0.72AU, 1.90m
 * Earth: 1AU, 2.65m
 * Mars: 1.53AU, 4.05m
 * (Asteroid belt: 2.3-3.3AU, 6.1-8.7m)
 * Jupiter: 5.2AU, 13.8m
 * Saturn: 9.6AU, 25.4m
 * Uranus: 18.7AU, 49.5m
 * Neptune: 30.2AU, 80m

Separately we can ask how large they should be, but I expect that will have to be a different scale otherwise
they will be mostly invisible.

Unfortunately I think having Mercury,Venus,Earth all in the first 3 metres means at least some of them will have to
be inside bushes.

There is a big bush from about 1.7 to 4.8 metres, which covers Venus, Earth, and Mars.

And annoyingly Saturn ends up in this bush if starting from the road and ending at the solar panels.

Starting from the road and ending at the very bottom looks like it would work? Not so keen on having to walk around
the back of the garage to see the majority of it though.

## Plasma cutter nozzle

My 1.1mm nozzles arrived so I tried it out.

It seemed to cut 3mm steel at 600 mm/min and I moved on to the 5mm steel.

I couldn't get a really good cut at any speed.

These are my attempts: https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6850

From top to bottom this is:

 * 150 mm/min: bottom side welded back together after jet has passed
 * 250 mm/min: same
 * 400 mm/min: not fully penetrated
 * 150 mm/min: welded back together
 * 100 mm/min: same
 * 300 mm/min: same

I.e. the bottom side was welded back together, with like extreme "low speed dross", unless travel
speed is too high, and then it doesn't penetrate.

Bottom sides are here ("top" is right of pic): https://img.incoherency.co.uk/6852

You can see that the underside of the cut looks basically identical regardless of
travel speed, except for the 400 mm/min one which didn't fully penetrate.

It seems like it's melting all the way through but somehow not making the molten metal detach?

So I thought maybe it needs more pressure so I cranked the pressure regulator all the way up, but
it drops to 4 bar by the time the compressor kicks on so I can't really increase pressure. I also
tried decreasing it to 3 bar but it didn't make any apparent difference at all.

Why would the molten metal not be detaching?

By the token that reducing the pressure didn't make it worse I am kind of guessing that increasing
the pressure wouldn't make it better, but obviously don't know for sure.

The very end of each cut is not covered with this "low speed dross", there's
a small hole where it finishes cutting, I wonder if that tells us anything? It's not to do with post-flow
because it reaches the end of the cut, switches off arc, and immediately travels back to origin.

Perhaps the jet is blowing the molten metal backwards along the cut and it is accumulating a bit behind the place
where it originally came from? Instead of detaching and falling off.

We could consider putting anti-spatter spray on the underside of the plate.

Also seems remiss not to test 350 mm/min, just in case faster travel does actually work. I'll quickly try that.

No dice, just the same identical-looking low-speed dross on the bottom.

I also tried cutting out a square at 150 mm/min just to see if it would detach or look different in different directions,
but I couldn't separate it even banging it really hard with a hammer.

What about running at a lower power setting? So we'd be adding heat more slowly for the same amount of air blowing?
